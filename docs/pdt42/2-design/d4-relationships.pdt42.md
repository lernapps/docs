# Choose the core relationships

*The core system of this iteration is the triangle of the three core roles: app creators, adopting adults and students. Most value flows between creators and adults and between adults and students (D3). The core entity is the adopting adults: without them, no app reaches a classroom and no attention reaches a creator.*

Creators are not the core entity because they build software without the platform anyway, as personal software for their own class or child. That others use their app too is a nice-to-have for them. Adults, in contrast, depend on finding apps they can use without doubt; this is where the platform makes the difference.

The brief (E7) named creators and adults as the core relationship and students as an ancillary role. The triangle widens this: students also look for apps on their own and return to them, and gratitude flows between all three roles. Every role in the triangle has a portrait (D2).

## Creators and adults

The relationship the brief started from. Creators give adults apps; adults give creators attention and could give feedback, recognition and gratitude. These last three are all potential today, and they are what creators lack.

```pdt42
:::relationship
id: r-creators-adults
title: Creators and adults
between: e-creators, e-adopters
core: yes
:::
```

## Adults and students

Adults bring apps to students by link, QR code or projector; students give teachers free time by working on their own. This is how most students get to know an app.

```pdt42
:::relationship
id: r-adults-students
title: Adults and students
between: e-adopters, e-students
core: yes
:::
```

## Creators and students

The apps are built for students, and students return to them on their own. Gratitude could flow in both directions.
